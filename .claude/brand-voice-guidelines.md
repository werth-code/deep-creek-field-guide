# Field Guide — brand voice guidelines

For delawarefieldguide.com, deepcreekfieldguide.com, and any field guide built
after them. Written 4 August 2026 from the voice audit plus a measurement pass
over the live copy.

Confidence: **high** on rules 1–6 (measured against published pages), **medium**
on the register notes in §5 (judgement, not data).

---

## The one-line brief

Matt talking to someone who asked him a question. Not Matt writing.

---

## 1. What actually still reads as AI

The audit fixed em dashes, tricolons and the missing "I". A measurement pass
over the rewritten About page found the remaining problem, and it isn't a word
choice.

**Every sentence is complete, purposeful, and pulling its weight.**

35 sentences on that page. One fragment. Zero parentheticals. Nothing starting
with *And* or *But* or *So*. No aside, no restatement, no throwaway. Every
clause earns its place.

That is what an editor produces. It is not what a person talking produces.
Real speech wastes a little, and the waste is the signal.

| Measured on /about/ before this doc | Count | Target |
|---|---|---|
| Parentheticals | 0 | 1 per ~400 words |
| Fragments (<5 words) | 1 of 35 | 3–4 of 35 |
| Sentences opening And/But/So | 0 | 2–3 per page |
| Contractions | 22 | keep |

## 2. The four tics to kill

These are mine, not generic. Each one is in the live copy.

**Antithesis.** Balanced opposition used as a closer. *"Not a local, but not a
stranger either."* *"A commitment to disclosure can be kept. A commitment to
poverty can't."* It sounds wise. It is a machine reaching for symmetry.
→ Say the thing once.

**The aphoristic closer.** Still doing it after the audit banned it.
*"Provenance is not the same as permission."* Any sentence that would work as a
pull quote should be deleted, because that is what makes it feel written.
→ End on the fact. Then stop.

**The `, which is …` chain.** *"…rather than on my impressions, and why most of
the photographs on it are mine."* Two subordinate clauses stacked to keep a
sentence going.
→ Full stop. New sentence.

**"Rather than".** Appears constantly. It is the connective tissue of an essay,
not of a person.
→ "instead of", or split the sentence, or cut it.

## 3. How to add the slack back

Not by writing worse. By writing the way the sentence would actually come out.

- **Start a sentence with And, But or So.** Two or three per page.
- **Use a parenthetical** for the thing you'd say in a lower voice. *(It was
  closed. Obviously.)*
- **Leave a fragment.** "Three hours from Baltimore. In a good way."
- **Restate once** when it matters. Repetition is emphasis in speech.
- **Include one detail that doesn't serve the argument.** The bugs. The car
  park being smaller than it looks. That is what proves you were there.
- **Let a sentence trail into an aside** instead of resolving it neatly.

## 4. Rules that carry over from the audit

Unchanged and still enforced:

- First person singular for anything I did. Never the royal "we".
- Em dashes: **1 per 300 words.** Currently 0 on both homepages. Keep it there.
- No three-item lists in consecutive paragraphs.
- Contractions on. "Don't", not "do not".
- Vary paragraph length hard. One-sentence paragraphs are allowed and good.
- Never open two pages the same way. Never open two *sites* the same way.
- Say what I don't know. Concrete numbers and real names, never "several".
- Dry, sparing humour. No puns, ever.

**Ban list:** pawsome · tail-wagging · fur-ever · hidden gem · nestled ·
boasts · a testament to · in today's world · whether you're X or Y · "the
perfect blend of" · "nature lovers" · "outdoor enthusiasts"

**Also banned, added here:** "leans on" · "earns its place" · "worth knowing" ·
"the honest version of" · "that's the deal here". All mine, all overused.

## 5. Register

British-inflected spelling is already inconsistent across the sites
("centre", "programme", "neighbourhood") against an American subject. Pick
American and hold it: **center, program, neighborhood, favorite, traveling.**
Regional words stay: *down the shore*, *downy ocean*, *Slower Lower*.

Matt is: an engineer, a photographer, someone who drives all over the US to
look at parks, and usually has kids in the car. He is not an outdoorsman
performing expertise. He wants the answer before loading the car, and assumes
you do too.

## 6. Two things that must never get casual

Conversational tone applies to prose. It does not apply to:

- **A fact and its source.** "Reservations required weekends and holidays,
  Memorial Day through Labor Day" stays exactly that precise. No warmth in the
  data.
- **An unknown.** "Not confirmed" is the wording. Never soften it into
  "probably", "should be", or "we think".

The voice is friendly. The evidence standard is not.
